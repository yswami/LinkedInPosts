    The critical distinction

    Agency is what the system can do. Autonomy is how independently it may do it in this context.

    Boundary: this evaluates allocation logic, not model accuracy, security, integration reliability, legal compliance, or production readiness.

    The autonomy-envelope method

    Five questions before granting more autonomy

    01

    Impact

    What happens financially, operationally or to people if the action is wrong?

    02

    Reversibility

    Can the organization safely undo the action, and at what cost?

    03

    Evidence

    Can the required facts be verified before the action is committed?

    04

    Coverage

    Does an approved rule cover this situation without hidden interpretation?

    05

    Novelty

    Is this a familiar pattern, or an exception the operating model has not learned yet?

    Research notes

    Why allocation should be stage-specific

    Automation has different stages

    Classic human-factors research separates information acquisition, analysis, decision selection and action implementation—each of which may use a different automation level.

    Read the IEEE paper

    Oversight depends on context

    NIST recommends documenting task scope, knowledge limits, risk tolerance, human–AI roles, oversight and intervention throughout the lifecycle.

    Explore the NIST AI RMF Core

    Agency and autonomy are different

    Emerging work distinguishes what an agent can do from how independently it may act, and describes checkpoints, escalation, tool fencing and staged writes as control tactics.

    Read the 2026 preprint

    This page synthesizes those ideas into an educational design exercise. The five-gate envelope is not presented as a validated universal scoring model.
