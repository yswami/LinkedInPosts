The experiment

Can the organization explain the work?

For each dimension, choose whether the current instruction defines it clearly. Then compare it with a clarified work contract.

1Outcome priority

Is it clear whether accuracy, payment speed, or supply continuity takes priority?

2Accountable owner

Can one role be identified as accountable for the final exception decision?

3Decision threshold

Does “material” have a measurable monetary, percentage, risk, or policy definition?

4Agent authority

Is it clear whether the agent may collect evidence, recommend, route, approve, or release payment?

5Exception path

When policy and business continuity conflict, is the override owner and evidence requirement defined?

6Success measure

Can the organization verify that the routing and final outcome were correct?

Organizational legibility 0%

STOP AND CLARIFY

Do not ask the agent to choose between three hidden priorities.

Appropriate agent response

“I found conflicting interpretations of the invoice exception. Please identify the accountable owner, materiality rule, permitted action, and exception path before I proceed.”

    The working hypothesis

    Agent readiness may begin with organizational legibility.

    Humans often bridge unclear ownership and rules through relationships, memory, judgement, and informal escalation. An agent needs those interfaces to become explicit.

    Research notes

    Why this is a hypothesis worth testing

    Role ambiguity already affects human work

    A meta-analysis found a modest negative association between role ambiguity and job performance. This is human evidence, not direct proof about agents.

    Read the meta-analysis

    Human + AI is an organization-design problem

    Research frames human–AI decision-making as a multi-agent organizational system involving division of labour, communication, interdependence, and feedback.

    Read the organization-design paper

    Governance requires explicit interfaces

    NIST calls for clear human–AI roles, task scope, oversight, metrics, testing, monitoring, and accountability throughout the system lifecycle.

    Explore the NIST AI RMF

    The inference being explored: organizational clarity may improve the probability of successful agent implementation. The cited evidence supports its components, but does not establish this complete causal claim.

    Clarity is not the same as rigidity.

    Roles and policies can change rapidly. The current outcome, ownership, authority, exception path, and effective version still need to be understandable at the moment of action.
