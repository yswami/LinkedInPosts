## First comment / research note

This series treats “organizational legibility” as a working design hypothesis—not a proven causal law or validated maturity model.

The approach connects several research and governance ideas:

• Role ambiguity is associated with work outcomes, although that literature does not by itself prove agent-implementation success.
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/014920630002600104

• Human–AI decision-making can be understood as an organization-design problem involving task allocation, coordination and control.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41469-021-00095-2

• Automation can be allocated differently across information acquisition, analysis, decision selection and action implementation.
https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354

• NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework calls for clear roles, differentiated human–AI oversight, documented limits, monitoring, appeal and override, recovery, and continual improvement.
https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/

My synthesis is practical: treat the organization surrounding an agent as part of its execution environment—and test both together.
