## Reader takeaway

Do not equate a capable agent with an organization ready to use one. The surrounding operating model needs an available owner, usable evidence, meaningful intervention, controlled execution and recovery, and a learning loop.

## First comment / research note

The five-signal drill is an educational synthesis, not a validated maturity model or certification.

It draws on NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework, which treats governance as a cross-cutting lifecycle function and calls for clear organizational roles and communications, differentiated human–AI oversight configurations, defined oversight processes, ongoing monitoring, appeal and override mechanisms, incident response, recovery, change management, and continual improvement:

- NIST AI RMF Core: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
- NIST AI RMF, GOVERN outcomes: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
- NIST AI RMF, MAP outcomes for human oversight: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
- NIST AI RMF, MANAGE outcomes for post-deployment monitoring and override: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
