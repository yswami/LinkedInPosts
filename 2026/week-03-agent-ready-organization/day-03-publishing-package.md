## Reader takeaway

Allocate autonomy by stage of work, not by whole role or job. The autonomy envelope should respond to consequence, reversibility, evidence sufficiency, policy coverage, and novelty.

## First comment / research note

The Day 3 model is an educational synthesis, not a validated assessment instrument.

Its foundation includes:

- Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens’ model of automation across information acquisition, information analysis, decision/action selection, and action implementation: https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354
- NIST AI RMF guidance to define human–AI configurations, oversight, knowledge limits, task scope, risk tolerances, and post-deployment intervention: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
- NIST’s Human–AI Interaction appendix, which notes that human–AI configurations can range from manual to autonomous and should be differentiated by context: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/appendices/app-c-ai-risk-management-and-human-ai-interaction/
- Emerging 2026 work distinguishes agency—what a system can do—from autonomy—how independently it may act—and describes checkpoints, escalation, tool fencing, and staged writes as control tactics: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12105
