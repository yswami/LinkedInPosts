Missing decision mechanics8 gaps
    Illustrative decision contract
    Trigger
    Mismatch exceeds $5,000 or 5%, or supply interruption is forecast within 72 hours.
    Evidence
    Invoice, purchase order, receipt, buyer note, variance calculation, and current supplier-risk signal.
    Recommend
    The agent assembles evidence and recommends route; it does not approve.
    Decide
    Procurement Manager may accept a commercial variance within policy; Finance decides a control override.
    Execute
    Accounts Payable releases only after the approval record passes payment controls.
    Limit
    No autonomous approval or release; any policy override requires two named human approvals.
    Escalate
    Finance plus business owner decide when supply continuity conflicts with payment control.
    Record
    Store evidence, recommendation, decision, reason, approvers, action, outcome, and policy version.

    A reusable method

    Run the two-person decision-contract test.

    Choose one exception with real consequences. Ask two experienced people to complete the eight fields independently. Compare answers before automating anything.

    1. 01
      Choose one decision

      Not a whole job. Use a specific trigger, such as approving an invoice variance.

    2. 02
      Write independently

      Two practitioners define evidence, decision rights, limits, escalation, and record.

    3. 03
      Compare the differences

      Disagreement is useful evidence of hidden organization design—not a prompt-engineering issue.

    4. 04
      Test the contract

      Use historical cases and edge conditions before granting the agent any action authority.

    Research notes

    Why the decision boundary matters

    Human–AI work requires a designed division of labour

    Organization-design research treats the human and algorithm as a goal-oriented system whose tasks must be decomposed, allocated, integrated, and supported by exception handling.

    Read the open-access paper

    AI governance differentiates roles and oversight

    NIST’s AI RMF calls for documented accountability structures, clear lines of communication, and policies that distinguish human–AI roles and oversight.

    Explore the NIST AI RMF Core

    Even RACI practitioners warn about decision rights

    A practitioner critique notes that RACI participation labels may still leave the final recommendation, decision authority, and veto unclear. This is supporting practice evidence, not a controlled study.

    Read the practitioner perspective

    The inference explored here is intentionally narrow: a role map may be useful yet insufficient for a machine-executable decision. The lab is an educational diagnostic, not a validated governance framework.

    Do not throw away the RACI.

    Keep it when it helps coordination. Add the decision contract where an agent—or a human—must know the trigger, evidence, authority, action, exception, and retained accountability record.
