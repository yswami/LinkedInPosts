The interactive playbook

Create a pilot card your team can understand.

Use the example, replace it with your own decision, and copy the result. Plain language is a feature here.

1

Pick one decision

Write it as a question. If it cannot fit in one sentence, make it smaller.

2

Explain it in five lines

This is the shared instruction for the team and the agent.

3

Divide the moves

Choose who leads each part. Do not automate the whole job at once.

4

Set the stop rules

When any checked condition occurs, the agent pauses and asks for help.

5

Rehearse a bad day

Try each failure before the pilot touches real work.

REHEARSE BEFORE PILOT

Your pilot fits on one page.

The decision and boundaries are clear. Rehearse all three failures before using it with live work.

Boundary: this is an educational planning aid, not legal, audit, security, risk, compliance, or production approval.

Why this order

Small enough to learn. Clear enough to operate.

Each step answers a question that a confident demo cannot answer for you.

01

Can we explain it?

If the decision is unclear to people, the agent inherits the ambiguity.

02

Can we stop it?

A human role matters only when someone can pause, question, or reverse the work in time.

03

Did it help?

More completed actions are not success. Better decisions and business outcomes are.

Do not expand authority because the demo looked good.

Let observed decisions, recoveries, and business outcomes earn the next move.
