One invoice began the series: eighty-four thousand six hundred dollars, a six-point-two percent mismatch, and forty-eight hours to act.

The question seemed simple: can the agent solve it? Each experiment exposed another organizational seam.

A vague instruction produced three answers. A RACI named people, but not the decision.

A clear decision still needed limits. A capable recommendation still needed an organization ready to act.

So: pick one decision. Explain it. Divide the moves. Set stop rules. Rehearse a bad day.

Then pilot it small, measure what happened, and let the results earn the next move.

The agent enters a human operating system. Organization design becomes part of its execution.

Clarity is not rigidity. Update the rules as work changes. Then ask: which decisions can humans and agents improve together?
