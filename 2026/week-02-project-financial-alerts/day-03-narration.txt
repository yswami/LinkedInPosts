At 8:25, Project Meridian has passed two controls. The ten-percent variance is validated, and policy says escalate. Then the agent writes: “The project will overrun by exactly eight million dollars.”

That sounds decisive. But eighty-eight million is an estimate at completion—not a final invoice. Validation confirms the forecast’s sources. It does not eliminate uncertainty.

The working EAC is eighty-eight million against an eighty-million budget. Supported scenarios run from eighty-five to ninety-two million. Confidence is medium because pending changes and remaining work can move the result.

A decision-ready explanation separates observed actuals, commitments, estimates, and assumptions. It names the range, major drivers, evidence date, and next decision.

Good AI does not hide uncertainty or manufacture confidence. It makes uncertainty usable: what we know, what may change, and what someone must decide next.
