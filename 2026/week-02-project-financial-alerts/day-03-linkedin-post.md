Day 3/5 — A validated forecast is still not a fact

At 8:25, Project Meridian has passed two controls.

Day 1: the +$8M cost variance was validated against its source records.
Day 2: the governed policy classified it as ESCALATE.

The agent now drafts this message:

“Project Meridian will overrun by exactly $8M.”

It sounds decisive. It is also stronger than the evidence.

The $88M estimate at completion contains different kinds of financial information:

• $46M of actual cost is observed and posted
• $42M of estimate to complete is still a forecast
• Commitments, pending changes and remaining-work assumptions may move that forecast

Validation establishes where the number came from. It does not turn an estimate into a final invoice.

A more decision-ready explanation would say:

ESCALATE — Current EAC is $88M against an $80M budget, a forecast variance of +$8M.

Supported planning scenarios currently span $85M–$92M. This is a scenario range, not a statistical confidence interval.

Confidence is medium. Actuals are current through 24 Aug; the main sensitivities are pending change treatment and the remaining-work estimate.

Finance review is required by Friday before any recovery action.

That explanation separates four things:

1. What is observed
2. What is estimated
3. What could change the estimate
4. What someone must decide next

If a supported range does not exist, the agent should say so and name the unresolved drivers—not manufacture one.

The takeaway: validation creates traceability. Good explanation makes the remaining uncertainty usable.
