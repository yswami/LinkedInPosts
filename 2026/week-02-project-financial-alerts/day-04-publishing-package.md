## Suggested video title

A recovery plan can improve one project and damage the portfolio

## Video accessibility description

A narrated Project Meridian example continues from a validated, escalated, and explained cost forecast. Moving two shared architects improves Meridian’s EAC and schedule, but raises Project Beacon’s EAC, delays its go-live, and moves a $12M milestone into the next quarter. The animation compares this local recovery with a phased-scope alternative and shows why recovery options must be evaluated across portfolio cost, schedule, cash timing, dependencies, capacity, and approvals.

## First comment

The Day 4 experiment begins with a recommendation that appears compelling on Project Meridian’s dashboard.

Try the three views:

1. “Judge Meridian only” shows a $4M local improvement.
2. “Add the direct dependency” exposes Project Beacon’s cost and schedule impact.
3. “Build the portfolio view” recalculates net EAC, reveals the $12M cash-timing effect, and compares an alternative.

The figures are fictional and intentionally deterministic. The point is not that one recovery technique is universally better. It is that a recommendation should disclose the trade-offs and governed assumptions used to compare options.

Which portfolio consequence is most often missing from project recovery discussions: shared capacity, cash timing, strategic priority, or downstream delivery risk?
