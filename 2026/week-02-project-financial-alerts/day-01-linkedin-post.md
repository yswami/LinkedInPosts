Day 1/5 — The $8M overrun that might not exist

At 8:05 on Monday morning, an AI agent reviews Project Meridian.

Approved cost budget: $80M
Actual cost: $46M
Estimate to complete: $42M
Estimate at completion: $88M

Potential overrun: $8M.

Should the agent alert the project manager?

Not yet.

The actuals were last refreshed 36 hours ago.

900 contractor hours are awaiting approval.

A $6M purchase-order commitment is visible—but its treatment in the current forecast has not been reconciled.

Exchange rates have also moved since the previous reporting cycle.

The $8M is therefore not one clean fact. It is an inference assembled from actuals, commitments, forecasts, currencies, accounting periods and data timestamps.

The agent has found a signal.

It has not yet found a decision-ready issue.

A responsible first response might be:

“Project Meridian shows potential cost exposure of $8M–$15.4M. The result is provisional because recent labor, commitment treatment and currency assumptions require validation.”

That is less dramatic than a red warning.

It is also more useful.

In enterprise PPM, the first agentic decision is not what action to take. It is whether the evidence is strong enough to interrupt someone—and whether the interruption should report an overrun or request data validation.
